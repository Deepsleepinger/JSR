\noindent
\textbf{Context and Motivation:}\\
In transient continuum mechanics, thermal transport, and multiphysics simulations, numerical time integration generates sequences of large, sparse symmetric positive definite linear algebraic systems $A_t x_t = b_t$. In problems characterized by moving phase-change fronts, localized thermal softening, non-Newtonian boundary layer evolution, or progressive damage, the physical differential operator evolves intensely across a localized sub-region while the vast background remains quasi-static. Traditional parallel domain decomposition methods face an expensive operational dilemma: recomputing all subdomain sparse direct factorizations at every time step prevents Krylov iteration inflation but incurs heavy setup overhead (often 25\%--45\% of total execution time in three dimensions); conversely, freezing the initial preconditioner statically costs zero setup but triggers severe spectral mismatch and solver divergence.

\vspace{0.8em}
\noindent
\textbf{Core Scientific Contributions:}\\
In this work, we introduce a stateful selective maintenance framework for Two-Level Overlapping Restricted Additive Schwarz (RAS) preconditioners accelerated by sparse direct subdomain solvers (PETSc/MUMPS) and adaptive coarse Galerkin projections. The key contributions include:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Persistent Stateful Architecture}: Rather than rebuilding from scratch or freezing statically, the preconditioner is maintained as a persistent stateful object where symbolic structures are cached and numerical refactorizations are targeted dynamically.
    \item \textbf{Inexpensive Online Monitoring}: An $\mathcal{O}(n_i)$ diagonal operator-drift proxy senses local perturbations with negligible computational overhead ($< 0.28\%$ of saved setup time), showing strong empirical agreement in subdomain ranking (Pearson $r = 1.0000$, Spearman $\rho_s \in [0.79, 0.98]$ on tested benchmarks).
    \item \textbf{Mass-$\alpha$ Cumulative-Drift Policy}: Disturbed subdomains are selected via a cumulative staleness metric with aging penalty, balancing local high-frequency correction while the coarse space maintains global error control.
    \item \textbf{Subdomain Granularity Resolution Effect}: A systematic study across $N_{\text{sub}} \in \{8, 27, 64\}$ quantifies how spatial decomposition resolution enables sharper front isolation, reducing the refactored ratio to $31.8\%$ and eliminating Krylov iteration penalty ($47.0$ vs. $47.0$ iterations).
    \item \textbf{Certified 3-D Flagship Speedup}: On a flagship 3-D benchmark with $110,592$ degrees of freedom ($48^3$), a 63.9\% reduction in preconditioner setup translates into up to a 21.8\% reduction in end-to-end simulation runtime ($1.24\times \sim 1.28\times$ net speedup), certified under an independent algebraic residual bound of $\text{RelRes} \le 3.89 \times 10^{-10} \ll 1.0 \times 10^{-8}$.
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{0.8em}
\noindent
\textbf{Delineation from Contemporary Literature:}\\
We clearly delineate our work from contemporary Krylov subspace recycling (e.g., Hanek, Pape\v{z} \& \v{S}\'{i}stek, \textit{CMAME} 452 (2026), 118788). While Krylov recycling deflates an invariant operator $A x_t = b_t$, our framework actively repairs the preconditioner to track a time-evolving operator $A_t x_t = b_t$ ($A_t \neq A_{t-1}$).
