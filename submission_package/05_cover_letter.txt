"Stateful Selective Maintenance of Two-Level Schwarz Preconditioners for Evolving Sparse Linear Systems"

Context and Motivation:
In transient continuum mechanics, thermal transport, and multiphysics simulations, numerical time integration generates sequences of large, sparse symmetric positive definite linear algebraic systems A_t x_t = b_t. In problems characterized by moving phase-change fronts, localized thermal softening, non-Newtonian boundary layer evolution, or progressive damage, the physical differential operator evolves intensely across a localized sub-region while the vast background remains quasi-static. Traditional parallel domain decomposition methods face an expensive operational dilemma: recomputing all subdomain sparse direct factorizations at every time step prevents Krylov iteration inflation but incurs heavy setup overhead (often 25%--45% of total execution time in three dimensions); conversely, freezing the initial preconditioner statically costs zero setup but triggers severe spectral mismatch and solver divergence.

Core Scientific Contributions:
In this work, we introduce a stateful selective maintenance framework for Two-Level Overlapping Symmetric Weighted Additive Schwarz (S-AS) preconditioners accelerated by sparse direct subdomain solvers (PETSc/MUMPS) and exact coarse Galerkin projections. The key contributions include:
1. Persistent Stateful Architecture & Conditional Spectral Stability: Rather than rebuilding from scratch or freezing statically, the preconditioner is maintained as a persistent stateful object. We establish a rigorous conditional perturbation theorem (Proposition 1) proving that bounded relative drift on skipped subdomains (||E_j||_2 <= eps < 1/2) strictly bounds the effective condition number by kappa(M_t^{-1} A_t) <= (1 / (1 - 2 eps)) kappa(M_exact^{-1} A_t).
2. Inexpensive Online Monitoring: An O(n_i) diagonal operator-drift proxy senses local perturbations with negligible computational overhead (< 0.026% of total runtime, < 0.28% of saved setup time), showing strong empirical agreement in subdomain ranking (Pearson r = 1.0000, Spearman rho in [0.79, 0.98] on tested benchmarks).
3. Mass-alpha Cumulative-Drift Policy & Coarse Synchronization: Disturbed subdomains are selected via a cumulative staleness metric, while coarse-space Galerkin synchronization preserves low-frequency error damping. Multi-step ablation demonstrates coarse synchronization is essential to prevent long-wave degradation.
4. Cross-Operator Generalization: We formalize the extension to vector continuum mechanics via 3-D damaged linear elasticity, establishing that the stateful maintenance framework is an algebraic operator strategy governed by the spatial sparsity of time-derivative support.
5. Subdomain Granularity Resolution Effect: A systematic study across N_sub in {8, 27, 64} quantifies how spatial decomposition resolution enables sharper front isolation, reducing the refactored ratio to 31.8% and eliminating Krylov iteration penalty (47.0 vs. 47.0 iterations).
6. Certified 3-D Flagship Speedup: On a flagship 3-D benchmark with 110,592 degrees of freedom (48^3), a 63.9% reduction in preconditioner setup translates into up to a 21.8% reduction in end-to-end simulation runtime (1.24x ~ 1.28x net speedup), certified under an independent algebraic residual bound of RelRes <= 3.89e-10 << 1.0e-8.

Delineation from Contemporary Literature:
We clearly delineate our work from contemporary Krylov subspace recycling (e.g., Hanek, Papez & Sistek, CMAME 452 (2026), 118788). While Krylov recycling deflates an invariant operator A x_t = b_t, our framework actively repairs the preconditioner to track a time-evolving operator A_t x_t = b_t (A_t != A_{t-1}).
